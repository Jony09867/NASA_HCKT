THE PROBLEM & MISSION

Why Microgravity Fire Safety Matters

THE CHALLENGE

Fire Behaves Counterintuitively in Orbit

On Earth, gravity drives natural buoyant convection: hot gases rise, drawing fresh oxygen to the base of the flame and creating the familiar teardrop shape. In microgravity (freefall), buoyancy ceases. Flames become rounded, spherical, and diffusion-dominated.

Crucially, low-speed forced ventilation—such as spacecraft environmental control loops—can supply just enough oxygen to sustain steady smoldering and flame spread, even in regimes where Earth fires would self-extinguish.

THE MISSION

Synthesizing Decades of Fragmented Data

NASA and international partners have conducted pioneering combustion research across drop towers, the Space Shuttle, Mir, Cygnus resupply vehicles, and the International Space Station's Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR).

However, these critical findings are dispersed across separate PDFs, raw data tables, and technical memorandums. Freefall Explorer unifies these investigations into an intuitive research tool to help researchers discover, compare, and understand verified combustion physics.
